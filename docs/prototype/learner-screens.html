01 — Placement

It stops when it knows, not when the questions run out

A fixed 40-question test wastes a beginner's time and under-measures an advanced learner. This one picks each item for maximum Fisher information at the learner's current estimate, so every question is the most informative one that could be asked next.

Each skill carries its own standard error and stops independently. Reading may settle in nine items while listening needs twenty — so reading stops, and the remaining items go where the uncertainty still is.

02 — Listening

Real recordings, and a cast you start to recognise

1,162 audio files came out of the source books and are attached to the lessons they belong to. Where a dialogue was printed rather than recorded, it is spoken by the course's own recurring characters — so Daniel is the same Daniel in unit 4 and unit 91.

Transcripts stay hidden until the learner has committed to an answer. Revealing them earlier turns a listening exercise into a reading one.

03 — Speaking

Told which sound, not just that it was wrong

A single number tells a learner nothing they can act on. The recording is transcribed, force-aligned against what they were meant to say, and scored phoneme by phoneme — so the feedback names the sound, the word it went wrong in, and what to do with the mouth.

Repeated misses build a pronunciation profile, and the profile generates drills. A learner who substitutes /w/ for /v/ gets that pair back until it holds.

04 — Paper practice

The work done on paper stops being invisible

Most learners still do a lot of their practice in a notebook, and until now none of it reached the app. Photograph the page and it is read, then marked by exactly the same grader that marks typed writing — same rubric, same feedback, same evidence toward mastery.

The confirmation step is not a formality. Handwriting recognition fails precisely on the letters a learner is least sure how to form. What the machine read and what the learner wrote are stored as two different things, and only the second is ever marked — being penalised for the reader's mistake, in the one place you can't argue back, would be worse than not being marked at all.

05 — Writing

Corrections quote your own words back

Marked on five dimensions — deliberately the four the exam examiner already uses, plus mechanics, so practice feedback and exam feedback speak one language instead of two private vocabularies.

It never returns a rewritten text. Hand a model an essay and it will happily give you a better essay, and nobody learns from prose they didn't write. Corrections come back as spans that quote the learner exactly, shown in place. A span that can't be found in what they actually wrote is dropped — an uncheckable correction reads as the app inventing mistakes.
